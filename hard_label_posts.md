## 1. "Lets be honest... how am I suppose to survive on 'Entry Level jobs' in the bay area?"

**Current label:** `question`

**Could have been:** `question` or `analysis`

The body walks through real numbers — rent at roughly $2.5k+/month (~$30k/year),
taxes cutting a $70k salary down to about $50k take-home, leaving $15–20k for
everything else — building toward an implicit conclusion that entry-level pay
doesn't work here. That's the same shape as the PG&E rates post, which is
labeled `analysis`. The difference: this one never explicitly asks anyone
anything, it reads as rhetorical venting rather than a question seeking
answers, which is why `question` still holds — but it's close enough that a
different reader could land on `analysis` instead.

## 2. "I don't understand the posts about affording the Bay Area?" vs. "It is very easy to live a good life in the bay area on a 'good' salary"

**Current labels:** `opinion` and `personal_experience`, respectively

**Could have been:** either label, for both posts

These two are structural twins: each uses one household's specific budget
($150k combined / $3,100 rent in one; $200k combined / $3,600 rent in the
other) to argue a general claim about Bay Area affordability. They landed on
different labels — one `opinion`, one `personal_experience` — despite doing
the same move. Worth naming as a place where the taxonomy's fuzziest edge (a
personal budget generalized into a claim about the world) produced two
different calls on nearly identical structures.

## 3. "To the idiot who drives at 130+ mph on 280 south..." vs. "Is it me or are Tesla drivers the shittiest drivers in the bay?"

**Current labels:** `personal_experience` and `opinion`, respectively

Same genre — a direct-address rant at a driver — but opposite labels, and for
a real reason: the 130-mph post stays narrow (one specific car, encountered
three times), while the Tesla post generalizes a single morning's U-turn into
a claim about an entire category of drivers. This is a good one to cite as
the decision rule *working*, not failing — the remove-the-ask/generalization
test gives a different, correct answer for each.

## 4. "San Jose has carried the Bay Area during the World Cup and emerged the biggest Winner"

**Current label:** `opinion`

**Could have been:** `opinion` or `analysis`

Cites a specific number — an estimated $600 million in economic activity,
"3 times as much" as the Super Bowl — which could read as evidence for an
`analysis`. But the number is decoration on a boosterish, celebratory post
("Great job San Jose," "Hopefully the region will..."), not the engine of an
argument. A clean application of "is the fact load-bearing, or just along for
the ride?"

## Also worth a look: a post that doesn't fit any of the four labels

**"Looking for testers: iOS app for PG&E Flex Pricing pilot program"** — currently
labeled `opinion`, but it's a project announcement and call for beta testers.
No judgment being expressed, no real question, no personal story, no argued
conclusion. This isn't a hard boundary call so much as a sign the post
shouldn't be in the dataset at all under this taxonomy — worth deciding
whether to relabel it, cut it, or note it as a known limitation.
